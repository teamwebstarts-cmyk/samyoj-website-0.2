Email the Follow-Up, WhatsApp the Reminder: A Two-Channel Cadence

By Samyoj Growth Team · 29 October 2026 · 6 min read

Quotes, contracts and catalogues belong in email; “did you see the quote?” belongs on WhatsApp. This post shows a simple cadence that uses Gmail for substance and WhatsApp for speed — with consent handled properly on both channels.

1. Email-Only Follow-Ups Die in Promotions. WhatsApp-Only Follow-Ups Have No Room for Detail.

Watch what happens on a typical ₹2 lakh proposal. The salesperson emails a 9-page PDF and waits. Nothing. They then forward the same PDF on WhatsApp — where it competes with 60 other chats and family groups, gets opened if they’re lucky, and can’t be searched sensibly two weeks later.

Each channel failed for a different reason. Email is where documents live but attention doesn’t. WhatsApp is where attention lives but documents don’t get read carefully. Businesses that grow use both, deliberately: email carries the substance, WhatsApp carries the nudge. The customer gets a document they can file and a reminder they cannot miss.

2. What Belongs on Email vs What Belongs on WhatsApp

Send it on email… Send it on WhatsApp…
Quotations, proforma invoices, contracts “Quote sent on email — worth a look?” nudges
Catalogues, spec sheets, company profiles One-page teasers with a single question
Formal onboarding, terms, payment schedules Appointment and payment reminders (“tomorrow 11 am, confirmed?”)
Anything either party may need to search later Anything that needs a reply in the next hour
One sentence rule for the nudge
The WhatsApp reminder should never repeat the email. It should reference it and do one job: “Hi Ramesh, sent the revised quote on email this morning — best to review before Thursday?” Attachments on WhatsApp are for the final signed copy, not the working document.

3. The 3-Touch Cadence, Day by Day

Here is the cadence that works for proposals, admission enquiries, dealer onboarding — almost any considered purchase. Adjust the day count to your sales cycle; keep the shape.

1

Day 0 — Email the substance

Send the full document from your connected Gmail/Workspace mailbox: subject line with the deal name, the PDF attached, one short paragraph on top. In the meeting or chat, tell them plainly: “Details on email, I’ll ping you here tomorrow.” You have just set up permission for the nudge.

2

Day 1 — WhatsApp the nudge

One line, inside the normal conversation. If they reply, the 24-hour window is open — resolve questions right there. If the discussion gets detailed, escalate on email again and repeat the loop.

3

Day 4 — Email the close-the-loop message

“Sharing the summary again in case it got buried. If the timing isn’t right this quarter, happy to check back next month.” This is polite, structured, and gives a graceful exit — which paradoxically gets more replies than a fourth WhatsApp ping.

Scale the same shape to cohorts
For many contacts at once, flip the emphasis: run the WhatsApp campaign as the primary touch on an approved template — then email the detailed document to those who replied. And if some contacts never open the thread again, a polite retargeting follow-up to the no-reply cohort — available directly in Samyoj’s campaign tools — stays inside consent rules.

4. Connecting Gmail: What “Send-Only” Means (and Why It Should Matter to You)

Samyoj connects to Gmail or Google Workspace through Google sign-in with a send-only permission. In plain words: Samyoj can send email as you, and it cannot read your inbox. Your existing mail, drafts, and contacts stay untouched — Google’s Limited Use policy governs this, and disconnecting the mailbox revokes the token while keeping your sent history.

Once connected, sending feels like normal email with three upgrades: you can insert merge fields (name, company, the thing they enquired about), reuse saved templates for recurring documents, and every send is gated on recorded email consent for that contact. Attachments are stored privately before the send attempt and expire after 24 hours — so nothing sensitive lingers on a server.

Sends from your real mailbox

5. Consent Notes for Both Channels

Two channels means two consent trails, and they are tracked separately on each contact in Samyoj:

  • Email consent gates every Gmail send. If a contact never agreed to receive email, the send fails before it starts — no accidental spam.
  • WhatsApp consent gates campaigns. Broadcasts only go to contacts with a recorded opt-in source and timestamp, and a STOP-style reply opts them out automatically.
  • One-to-one WhatsApp replies are different: within the 24-hour customer-service window you can reply free-form to whoever messaged you — that’s the difference between responding and soliciting.

Keeping these trails separate isn’t bureaucracy; it’s what lets you message confidently without waking up to a number-quality warning from Meta or a spam folder reputation problem in Gmail. 6. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is it okay to send business documents on WhatsApp instead of email?

For quick final copies, yes — WhatsApp handles images, PDFs and documents fine in one-to-one chats. But working documents like quotations, contracts and spec sheets belong on email, where they can be searched, filed, forwarded internally and printed without scrolling a chat history. The two-channel cadence exists because each channel is weak where the other is strong.

Does Samyoj read my Gmail inbox?

No. The Gmail integration is send-only: Samyoj can send email from your connected mailbox with your permission, and it has no ability to read, label or sync your inbox. The connection is covered by Google’s API Services User Data Policy (Limited Use), and disconnecting revokes the token immediately.

Can I run bulk email campaigns to all my contacts from Samyoj?

No — and that is deliberate. Samyoj’s email channel is for one-to-one follow-ups and template-based sends to contacts with recorded email consent, not bulk email blasts. For reaching many people at once, WhatsApp campaigns on approved templates are the built-in path, because they carry Meta’s template quality controls and per-contact opt-out handling.

When should I follow up on email vs on WhatsApp?

Email when you are adding substance — a revision, a summary, a document. WhatsApp when you want a fast human response — a confirmation, a yes/no, a time check. If your last substantive touch was an email, the next touch is a one-line WhatsApp nudge; if the last touch was WhatsApp and the question got complex, move the detail back to email.

How do I know if someone has opened my email?

You don’t — Samyoj does not track email opens, and honest tools avoid it: Gmail acknowledges a send as “Accepted”, not “Read”, and pixel-based open tracking is unreliable at best. This is exactly why the cadence pairs email with WhatsApp: the WhatsApp reply is your read receipt.
