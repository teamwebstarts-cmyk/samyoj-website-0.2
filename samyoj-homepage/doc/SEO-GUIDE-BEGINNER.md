A plain-language guide for business owners and team members who want to understand SEO and take action — no technical jargon, just clear steps.

---

## Part 1: What Is SEO and Why Does It Matter?

SEO stands for **Search Engine Optimization**. It means making your website easy for Google to find, understand, and show to people who are searching for what you offer.

Think of it like a physical shop:
- **On-site SEO** = Making sure your shop is clean, well-organized, has clear signs, and customers can easily find what they need.
- **Off-site SEO** = Getting other people to talk about your shop, recommend it, and point customers your way.
- **Google Business Profile** = Your shop's listing on Google Maps — the box that shows up when someone searches "WhatsApp CRM near me."

If you do all three well, Google puts you at the top of search results. That means more people find you, visit your site, and become customers.

## Part 2: On-Site SEO — Things You Can Fix on Your Website

On-site SEO means everything that happens **on your website**. Here is a checklist of things to do, ordered from easiest to most involved.

### 2.1 Fix Your Meta Tags (5 minutes per page)

Every page on your website has a **title** and **description** that show up in Google search results. Think of them like a shop sign and a window display.

**What to check:**
- Does every page have a unique title that includes the word "Samyoj" and what the page is about?
- Does every page have a description that explains what the page is about in 1–2 sentences?
- Are the descriptions at least 150 characters long?

**Pages that need fixing right now:**
- All 5 legal pages (privacy policy, terms, cookie policy, refund policy, acceptable use policy) — their descriptions are too short (e.g., "Samyoj Privacy Policy."). Rewrite them to be 2–3 sentences explaining what the page covers.
- Contact page — add `meta keywords` tag.

### 2.2 Add Missing Social Media Tags (10 minutes)

When someone shares your page on WhatsApp, Facebook, or LinkedIn, a **preview card** appears. You control what that preview looks like using Open Graph (OG) tags and Twitter Card tags.

**What to check:**
- Does every page have `og:title`, `og:description`, `og:image`, and `og:url`?
- Does every page have a Twitter Card tag?
- Is the Twitter Card type `summary_large_image` (bigger preview = more clicks)?

**Pages that need fixing:**
- All 5 legal pages — missing Twitter Card tags and incomplete OG tags.
- Contact page — Twitter Card is `summary` (small) instead of `summary_large_image`.

### 2.3 Fix the Language Tag (2 minutes)

Your homepage uses `lang="en"` but every other page uses `lang="en-IN"`. Pick one and use it everywhere. Since you are an Indian company, use `lang="en-IN"` on all pages.

### 2.4 Add `hreflang` Tags (15 minutes)

If your website could be found by people searching in different countries or languages, you need `hreflang` tags. These tell Google which language/region your page is for.

**What to do:**
- Add `<link rel="alternate" hrefLang="en-IN" href="https://samyoj.com/page" />` to every page.
- Add `<link rel="alternate" hrefLang="x-default" href="https://samyoj.com/" />` to every page.

### 2.5 Fix Broken Links (30 minutes)

Broken links send visitors to a dead page (404 error). Google hates them and so do your customers.

**Broken links found on your site:**
- On `features.html`, several buttons say "Explore" or "Learn more" but link to `#` (nowhere). Replace these with real links to relevant pages or remove them.
- Check all other pages for `href="#"` links that do nothing.

### 2.6 Add a Pricing Page (1–2 hours)

This is one of the biggest missing pieces. Your competitors show pricing. You don't. A pricing page:
- Helps visitors qualify themselves before contacting you
- Reduces unnecessary sales calls
- Gives Google more content to index
- Builds trust through transparency

**What to include:**
- Your Free Forever Plan details
- Paid plan pricing (₹1,500–₹45,000/month range from your schema)
- What each plan includes
- A clear CTA button for each plan
- FAQ section addressing common pricing questions

### 2.7 Create a Blog / Resource Center (Ongoing)

A blog is the single best way to get found on Google for questions people are searching for. Every blog post is a new page Google can index.

**Blog topic ideas for Samyoj:**
1. "What is WhatsApp CRM and Why Every Indian Business Needs One in 2026"
2. "WhatsApp Business API vs WhatsApp Business App: Which Should You Choose?"
3. "How to Automate Customer Support on WhatsApp (Step-by-Step)"
4. "WhatsApp Broadcast vs WhatsApp Groups: What's the Difference?"
5. "Click-to-WhatsApp Ads: How to Get Leads Directly Into WhatsApp"
6. "WhatsApp Marketing ROI: Real Numbers from Indian Businesses"
7. "How to Choose the Right WhatsApp CRM for Your Business"
8. "WhatsApp Business API Pricing in India: What You Actually Pay"
9. "5 WhatsApp Automation Workflows That Save 10+ Hours Per Week"
10. "WhatsApp CRM for E-Commerce: How to Recover Abandoned Carts"

**How often:** Aim for 1–2 blog posts per month. Quality over quantity.

### 2.8 Add Internal Links Between Pages

Internal links are links from one page on your site to another page on your site. They help Google understand your site structure and help visitors navigate.

**What to do:**
- From the homepage, link to `/features`, `/industries`, `/contact`, and `/pricing` in the body text (not just the navigation menu).
- From blog posts, link back to relevant pages on your site.
- Add a breadcrumb trail (Home > Features > Broadcast) so visitors and Google know where they are.

### 2.9 Add a Skip-to-Content Link

This is a small accessibility improvement that also helps SEO. Add a hidden link at the top of every page that jumps to the main content when pressed. Screen readers and keyboard users love it.

### 2.10 Fix Color Contrast Issues

Some text on your site is too light to read easily. This affects both accessibility and SEO (Google prefers accessible sites).

**Problem colors to fix:**
- Light gray text on white backgrounds (contrast ratio too low)
- Green accent text on white (borderline for normal-sized text)

Use a contrast checker tool (like WebAIM's free tool at webaim.org/resources/contrastchecker/) to verify your colors meet the WCAG AA standard (minimum 4.5:1 for normal text).

Off-site SEO means building your reputation and authority **outside** your website. Google looks at what other sites say about you to decide how trustworthy you are.

- Description: Write a 750-character description that includes keywords like "AI-powered WhatsApp CRM," "WhatsApp Business API," "official WhatsApp BSP," and "India"

**Step 4: Post Weekly**

**Step 5: Use GBP Q&A**

- Encourage customers to ask and answer questions
- This creates fresh content and shows Google your profile is active

### 3.2 Get Listed in Online Directories (Free, 1–2 hours)

Every time your business name, address, and phone number (called NAP) appears on another website, it builds trust with Google. These are called **citations**.

**Free directories to list Samyoj in:**
- JustDial (very important for Indian businesses)
- Sulekha
- IndiaMART
- TradeIndia
- LinkedIn Company Page (make sure it's complete)
- Clutch.co (if you have client testimonials)
- GoodFirms
- G2 (if you have reviews)

**Important rule:** Your NAP (Name, Address, Phone) must be **exactly the same** on every directory. Even small differences (like "Samyoj" vs "Samyoj Technologies" or "+91-8839888531" vs "918839888531") confuse Google and hurt your rankings.

### 3.3 Build Backlinks (Ongoing, Most Important Off-Site SEO)

A backlink is when another website links to yours. Google treats backlinks as "votes of confidence." The more high-quality votes you have, the more trustworthy Google thinks you are.

**Free ways to get backlinks:**

1. **Chamber of Commerce membership** — Join your local chamber (Indore Chamber of Commerce). They have a member directory with a link to your site. Cost: ₹100–₹500/year.

2. **Write guest posts** — Find business blogs in India that accept guest articles. Write a helpful article about WhatsApp marketing or automation and include a link to your site in the author bio or article body.

3. **Get featured in local news** — If Samyoj does something newsworthy (new product, partnership, milestone), reach out to local journalists and bloggers. A news article with a link is worth more than 100 directory listings.

4. **Sponsor local events** — Sponsor a local tech meetup, startup event, or business conference. Event pages almost always link to sponsors.

5. **Create link-worthy content** — Write a free guide like "The Complete Guide to WhatsApp Marketing in India 2026" and share it. Other websites will link to it as a resource.

6. **Partner with complementary businesses** — If you work with web design agencies, digital marketing firms, or IT consultants in India, ask them to link to your site from their "partners" or "recommended tools" page.

**What NOT to do:**
- Never buy backlinks. Google penalizes sites that buy links.
- Never use automated link-building tools.
- Never exchange links purely for SEO (link farms).

### 3.4 Social Media Presence

You already have LinkedIn, Instagram, and YouTube (listed in your Organization schema). Here is how to use them for SEO:

- **LinkedIn:** Post weekly about WhatsApp marketing tips, customer success stories, and product updates. Link back to your website.
- **YouTube:** Your product demo video is there. Add a description with keywords and link to your website in the description.
- **Instagram:** Share customer testimonials, behind-the-scenes content, and product tips. Link to your website in your bio.

### 3.5 Monitor Your Backlinks

Use **Google Search Console** (free) to see who is linking to your site. Go to the "Links" section and check:
- How many sites link to you
- Which pages get the most links
- Whether any suspicious sites are linking to you (toxic backlinks)

If you see spammy links, you can use Google's "Disavow" tool to tell Google to ignore them.

## Part 4: Google Search Console — How to Set It Up (Step-by-Step)

Google Search Console (GSC) is a **free** tool from Google that shows you exactly how Google sees your website. It is the single most important SEO tool you will ever use.

### Step 1: Go to Google Search Console

1. Open your browser and go to: **https://search.google.com/search-console**
2. Sign in with your Google account (use your business email, not a personal Gmail if possible)

### Step 2: Add Your Property

1. Click the **"Add Property"** button
2. You will see two options:
   - **Domain property** — covers everything under samyoj.com (recommended)
   - **URL prefix property** — covers only https://samyoj.com/
3. Choose **Domain property** and enter: `samyoj.com`

### Step 3: Verify Ownership

Google needs to prove you own the website. You have several options:

**Easiest method for your site (HTML file upload):**
1. In GSC, select "HTML file upload"
2. Google will give you a file to download (something like `google1234567890abcdef.html`)
3. Upload this file to the root directory of your website (the same folder where `index.html` lives)
4. The file should be accessible at: `https://samyoj.com/google1234567890abcdef.html`
5. Go back to GSC and click "Verify"

**Alternative method (DNS TXT record):**
1. In GSC, select "DNS record"
2. Google will give you a TXT record value
3. Log in to your domain registrar (where you bought samyoj.com)
4. Add the TXT record to your DNS settings
5. Wait a few hours for it to propagate
6. Go back to GSC and click "Verify"

### Step 4: Submit Your Sitemap

1. In GSC, look at the left sidebar
2. Click **"Sitemaps"** (under the "Indexing" section)
3. In the text box, type: `sitemap.xml`
4. Click **"Submit"**
5. You should see a message saying "Sitemap submitted successfully"

### Step 5: Request Indexing for Important Pages

1. In GSC, look at the left sidebar
2. Click **"URL Inspection"** (under the "Indexing" section)
3. Paste the URL of a page you want Google to index (e.g., `https://samyoj.com/features`)
4. Press Enter
5. Click **"Request Indexing"**
6. Repeat for: `/industries`, `/contact`, `/legal/privacy-policy`, etc.

### Step 6: Check Your Core Web Vitals

1. In GSC, look at the left sidebar
2. Click **"Experience"** (under the "Indexing" section)
3. You will see Core Web Vitals data for your pages
4. Green = good, Red = needs fixing

### What to Check in GSC Every Week

- **Performance:** Which pages get the most clicks and impressions? Which keywords bring traffic?
- **Indexing:** Are there any pages Google couldn't index? Fix those errors.
- **Experience:** Are your Core Web Vitals passing? If not, prioritize those fixes.
- **Links:** Who is linking to your site? Are there any toxic links?

### How Long Until You See Data?

- It takes **24–48 hours** for GSC to start showing data after setup.
- It takes **2–4 weeks** for Google to crawl and index new pages.
- Ranking changes usually take **4–8 weeks** to appear after making improvements.

## Part 5: How to Grab More Information About Your Site

### 5.1 Google Search Console (Free)

GSC tells you:
- Which keywords people use to find your site
- How many times your site appears in search results (impressions)
- How many people click through to your site (clicks)
- Your average position in search results
- Which pages have indexing errors
- Your Core Web Vitals scores
- How many backlinks you have

### 5.2 Google Analytics 4 (Free)

GA4 tells you:
- How many visitors come to your site
- Where they come from (Google search, direct, social media, etc.)
- What pages they visit and how long they stay
- Whether they complete actions (like clicking a CTA or filling a form)
- Which devices they use (mobile vs desktop)

**How to set it up:**
1. Go to https://analytics.google.com
2. Create a new property for samyoj.com
3. Add the tracking code to your website (ask your developer or add it to the `<head>` of your HTML files)
4. Wait 24–48 hours for data to appear

### 5.3 Google Business Profile Insights (Free)

Your GBP dashboard shows:
- How many people viewed your profile
- How many clicked "Call," "Directions," or "Visit Website"
- What search terms people used to find you
- What photos got the most views

## Part 7: Common SEO Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Don't keyword stuff** — Don't repeat "WhatsApp CRM" 50 times on one page. Write naturally for humans first.
2. **Don't copy content** — All your content should be original. Google penalizes copied content.
3. **Don't ignore mobile** — Most Indian users search on mobile. If your site doesn't work well on phones, you lose customers.
4. **Don't buy backlinks** — Google penalizes sites that buy links. Earn them naturally.
5. **Don't neglect your GBP** — An incomplete GBP profile means you miss out on local search traffic.
6. **Don't expect overnight results** — SEO takes 3–6 months to show real results. Be patient and consistent.
7. **Don't stop after setup** — SEO is ongoing. Check GSC weekly, post on GBP weekly, and publish blog content monthly.
8. **Don't ignore technical issues** — Slow loading, broken links, and missing meta tags all hurt your rankings. Fix them promptly.

## Part 8: Glossary of Common SEO Terms

- **SEO** — Search Engine Optimization. Making your website rank higher in Google search results.
- **On-site SEO** — Optimizing things on your website (content, meta tags, speed, structure).
- **Off-site SEO** — Building your reputation outside your website (backlinks, directories, reviews).
- **Keywords** — The words or phrases people type into Google when searching.
- **Meta title** — The clickable headline that appears in Google search results.
- **Meta description** — The short description under the title in Google search results.
- **Sitemap** — A file that lists all the pages on your website so Google can find them.
- **Indexing** — When Google adds your page to its database so it can show up in search results.
- **Backlink** — A link from another website to yours. Google treats these as "votes of confidence."
- **Core Web Vitals** — Google's metrics for measuring page speed, interactivity, and visual stability.
- **LCP (Largest Contentful Paint)** — How long it takes the main content of your page to load. Target: under 2.5 seconds.
- **INP (Interaction to Next Paint)** — How quickly your page responds when you click or tap something. Target: under 200 milliseconds.
- **CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift)** — How much your page layout shifts while loading. Target: under 0.1.
- **Google Business Profile (GBP)** — Your business listing on Google Maps and search results.
- **NAP** — Name, Address, Phone number. Consistency across directories is important for local SEO.
- **hreflang** — A tag that tells Google which language/region your page is for.
- **Canonical tag** — A tag that tells Google which URL is the "main" version of a page (prevents duplicate content issues).
- **Structured data / Schema** — Special code that helps Google understand what your page is about (e.g., reviews, pricing, FAQ).
- **CrUX** — Chrome User Experience Report. Google's real-world performance data from actual Chrome users.
- **GEO** — Generative Engine Optimization. Optimizing your content to be cited by AI engines like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini.
